# OT Communication Mechanisms and Associated Service Endpoints

This reference summarizes industrial control, building automation, industrial IoT, and physical-security communication mechanisms covered by the project's network-service collection inventory. It includes protocols, product-specific interfaces, and associated management or discovery services; these are **not** all distinct industrial protocols.

**How to read the tables**

- **Common endpoint(s)** identifies standard assignments or documented vendor defaults, as qualified in each row. `TCP/502` and `UDP/502`, for example, are different transport endpoints. A port registered by IANA is not necessarily an implementation's default or evidence that a particular protocol is present.
- **Project collection endpoint(s)** reproduces the endpoints listed in the source inventory. This column documents the project's stated collection scope, **not** a standards requirement, proof of a live deployment, or confirmation that every response was correctly identified at the application layer.
- Ports and services may vary by device, firmware, configuration, and protocol binding. Serial and Ethernet Layer 2 mechanisms do not have TCP/UDP service ports. A shared port or an HTTP interface alone does not establish a specific device or protocol identity.
- **Classification** is descriptive, not a formal standardization category. *Proprietary* means that the relevant interface or protocol is vendor-specific; it does not imply that the specification is necessarily secret.

## Interpretation and verification limits

The **project collection endpoint(s)** are transcribed from the source table and have not been independently compared with scanner configuration, service probes, packet captures, or deployment logs. They should therefore be understood as *reported collection configuration*, rather than independently verified observations. In particular, a successful TCP connection or UDP response is not sufficient to establish application-layer protocol identity. Any published measurements should document the actual probe, response validation criteria, collection period, and protocol-version coverage.

The rows for **DDP, ProConOS, legacy CODESYS endpoints, and some product-specific services** describe documented or reported implementations rather than universal industry standards. Product firmware and vendor documentation should be checked before operational use. The source table's unsupported equivalence between **Mitsubishi TCP/5007** and **MC Protocol/SLMP** has been corrected to the documented MELSOFT engineering interface. **PROFINET IO** and a device's **HTTP service** remain separate rows intentionally.

**Reference for registered services:** [IANA Service Name and Transport Protocol Port Number Registry](https://www.iana.org/assignments/service-names-port-numbers). IANA registration records an assignment; it is not an interoperability certification, a transport-implementation specification, or a method of device identification.
